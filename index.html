Know the patterns

Most scams leave clues.

One sign alone doesn’t prove a message is fraudulent. Several together are a reason to stop and verify independently.

⏱

Artificial urgency

Threats, deadlines, or “act now” demands are meant to make you react before you think.

🔑

Requests for secrets

Be cautious of unexpected requests for passwords, verification codes, payment details, or recovery phrases.

🔗

Unusual links or payment

Lookalike web addresses, unfamiliar payment methods, and requests to move money deserve extra scrutiny.

A calmer next step

Pause. Verify. Then decide.

Don’t rush

Stop before clicking, replying, sharing information, or sending money.

Check another way

Use a website or phone number you find independently—not details in the message.

Protect your accounts

If you already shared information, change affected passwords and contact your bank or service provider through official channels.

When in doubt, take a beat.

A legitimate request can wait while you verify it. A little time and a separate check can help you avoid a costly mistake.
